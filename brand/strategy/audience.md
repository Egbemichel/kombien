# Audience

**Status: approved working assumption.** Not validated with formal user research yet — that's expected for a project at this stage — but it's the reference to design and write copy against, not an open question. Update it as real user feedback comes in.

## Primary audience: passengers

People who regularly take shared or private taxis and negotiate fares informally — most immediately in urban Cameroon (Yaoundé, Douala, and similar cities), though the model generalizes to anywhere fare negotiation is informal and route-based.

What they need:

- A quick sense of what a route "should" roughly cost before they start negotiating.
- Confidence that the number isn't a stranger's guess — recency and volume of reports matter.
- A way to contribute back after a ride, with minimal friction (a destination, a price, maybe a note).

## Secondary audience: contributors and drivers

- **Contributors** — developers, designers, and translators who want to work on a project with real cultural grounding and are motivated by open source, not by equity or salary.
- **Drivers** — not a primary user of the app, but someone the brand and product should never mock or antagonize. Kombien is about passenger information, not adversarial pricing pressure.

## Tertiary audience: the open-source/civic-tech community

People evaluating whether to fork, contribute to, or reference Kombien as an example of community-sourced local data. This audience cares about clear documentation, licensing clarity, and a legible contribution path — see [../../CONTRIBUTING.md](../../CONTRIBUTING.md).

## What this means for design and copy

- Default to French/English/Franglish-aware copy, not English-only — see [../voice/franglais-guidance.md](../voice/franglais-guidance.md).
- Keep the core interaction (look up a route, report a fare) fast and low-friction; don't over-design the primary flow.
- Documentation aimed at contributors can be more technical and English-default, since that's the working language of most open-source tooling — but should stay welcoming to non-native English speakers.
